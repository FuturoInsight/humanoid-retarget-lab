Why it matters for data collection

Human demonstrations become robot training data only if the robot can reproduce them. This project checks, per clip, whether the human reached outside the robot's workspace, bent past its waist limit, moved faster than its actuators, or drove its hand into its own torso, and issues a verdict: usable, usable with clamping, or not feasible.

What is inside

  • MB-Lab character built headless in Blender 4.0; CMU BVH retargeted with per-bone rest-pose correction.
  • Robot retargeting by rotation decomposition onto joint axes, plus damped-least-squares IK.
  • Eight kinematics checks (FK error, position and velocity limits, clamping distortion, reach envelope, foot sliding, self-collision, jitter).
  • LeRobot-style export for a downstream QA pipeline; 45 unit tests that run without Blender.

The robot is a generic, hand-specified skeleton, not any real robot; verdicts demonstrate the method.
